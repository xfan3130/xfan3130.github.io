AP Psychology memory game · 3 to 6 players · 20 to 40 minutes

False Memory

Study the evidence. Trust your memory. Bet on it. One player is the Interrogator, and their job is to make you remember something that never happened.

Players

Goal

Finish with the most points after 6 cases. The role of Interrogator rotates each case. Play on one shared screen.

Each case

  1. Study. Everyone watches the evidence: a word list, a scene, or a set of letters.
  2. Lead. The Interrogator picks one false detail and reads the leading line aloud.
  3. Distract. Everyone counts backward out loud for 10 seconds.
  4. Testify. Each witness privately answers 4 questions and bets on each one.
  5. Debrief. The true answers and the psychology term appear.

Scoring

For each answer, bet your confidence: 1, 2, or 3.

  • A right answer gains your bet.
  • A wrong answer loses your bet.
  • The Interrogator does not testify.
  • The Interrogator gains a witness's full bet whenever that witness accepts the planted detail.

Scores can go below zero. High confidence is only safe if your memory is accurate.

Fair play

No notes and no talking during the test. Look away while another witness is testifying. The Interrogator must read the line exactly as written, but may use any tone of voice.

Sample case

  1. Everyone watches 12 words: bed, rest, tired, dream, and more. The word "sleep" never appears.
  2. Jordan is the Interrogator and chooses "sleep." Jordan reads aloud: "Remember seeing the word sleep on that list?"
  3. Everyone counts backward from 87 by 3s for 10 seconds.
  4. Mia testifies alone. She answers Yes to "Was sleep on the list?" and bets 3. She answers 3 more questions the same way.
  5. The debrief shows that sleep was not on the list. Mia loses 3 and Jordan gains 3. The term is false memory.
